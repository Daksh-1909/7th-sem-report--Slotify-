\chapter{Literature Survey}
\section{Practical}
Appointment scheduling is a critical part of service-based businesses, and many existing systems focus on automating booking while improving customer convenience. Popular platforms such as Calendly, Acuity Scheduling, Setmore, and Google Calendar are widely used for appointment management, but they are often designed for general-purpose scheduling rather than for local service businesses with complex staff availability, walk-in customers, and multi-service operations.

A common limitation in many existing solutions is that they do not fully support the operational needs of small service providers. For example, a salon, clinic, or wellness centre may require a system that handles multiple staff members, multiple service durations, customer reminders, and real-time slot availability. Some tools provide basic scheduling but do not prevent double booking effectively at the database level. Others allow bookings but lack a proper business owner dashboard, strong analytics, or business-specific workflow management.

Several well-known appointment systems offer online booking and reminder features, but they often focus on simplicity and general usage rather than multi-tenant business management. Local businesses also face challenges such as no-shows, dead slots, manual rescheduling, and poor visibility into repeat trends. This makes appointment booking a problem not only of convenience but also of operational efficiency and revenue management.

The literature around scheduling systems and business operations highlights the importance of real-time coordination, automated communication, and reliable data handling. In service-based organizations, poor scheduling creates delays, lost revenue, and customer dissatisfaction. Therefore, a robust scheduling system must provide both customer convenience and business-side control.
